ere it is told how a grey-furred cat, armed with a short sword, held off an army of pastry across eight trials. The scribe advises: the first trials are gentle on purpose — use them to learn the reach of the blade, because from the fifth onward the macarons do not wait. Relics fall in the middle of the field every fifteen heartbeats — take them before they fade. Beyond the eighth seal the codex turns to the siege: a castle stands in the meadow, the desserts march on its walls, and if the walls come down the day is lost however well the cat fights.
